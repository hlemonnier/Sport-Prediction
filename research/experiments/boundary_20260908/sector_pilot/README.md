Within TimingData, the physical packet ordinal determines update order. The replay clock is the cumulative maximum of recorded packet time. Auxiliary status/tyre records enter state only strictly before that clock; equal-time auxiliary updates are unavailable. This is an explicitly assumed cross-stream archive-time ordering, not a recovered client-receipt ordering or a network watermark guarantee. Current download receipt times are not historical race-day receipts. A prospective collector must persist UTC/monotonic client receipt, stream sequence and reconnect gaps separately.

The implementation snapshots all fields from one driver's packet atomically. Empty sector updates erase the latest raw value; first issued values remain immutable. Counter/sector collisions, late sectors near boundaries, reversals, jumps, missing S1 and ambiguous prior S1 are retained and flagged. Future timestamp-valid malformed auxiliary payloads keep their future time; malformed auxiliary timestamps stop that cursor rather than inventing a past placement. Late auxiliary status/tyre values preserve their source times and mark candidate eligibility ambiguous. These details were independently reviewed and tested before source lock and actual replay.

The raw information is useful, but its meaning must remain precise. Every candidate has an observed counter, position, pit state, TrackStatus and received candidate-stint Compound/New/StartLaps/TotalLaps fields. A current-slot missingness count is **not** evidence that historical sector data was never received. SpeedFL is also absent from the saved latest-field snapshots. S2 and SpeedI2 are normally unavailable at S1 issuance, as expected.

The highest observed TimingAppData stint record is saved as a candidate, not declared the active stint. Its TotalLaps is not relabeled canonical TyreLife. Before expanding to the remaining discovery streams, the concrete blockers are engineering and estimand contracts:

1. Freeze a prefix-preserving history of observed completed sector groups. The current ledger deliberately saves latest received fields, not a canonical completed-lap database. A strong remaining-sector baseline needs historical positive S2/S3 values retained across clears, with the time at which each group became usable. Counter/S3 collisions, delayed revisions, missing sectors and pit transitions must remain explicit. Later packets may update future history, but never rewrite earlier snapshots.
2. Choose an input contract that can actually be reproduced. A reduced raw-prefix feature set can support research without exact canonical HGB parity. Do not block that experiment merely because final IsAccurate is absent; instead, keep its historical-target predicate separate and declare the changed baseline input contract. Do not silently use final same-race CSV labels to create an allegedly live historical clean-lap buffer.
3. Freeze issuance, label existence and comparison units before scoring. A first experiment can use S2 as its primary checkpoint and S1 as a separately reported population. Every baseline and candidate must share each issued checkpoint. Retain cold starts using declared historical priors, and retain unmatched outcomes. Conditional point error must be labeled conditional on a later eligible completion. Weight by event and also by distinct eligible target so multiple sector forecasts for one outcome do not dominate. Do not infer current-versus-later canonical lap identity until that link is separately audited.
4. Retain the archive-time limitation. More downloads cannot manufacture historical client receipts, certify mirror archival immutability or prove equal-time cross-stream order. Freeze a recorded-time replay contract and any fixed delay sensitivities before scoring. Collect true receipt logs separately for a deployment claim.

The minimum useful baseline feature contract is: current observed S1/S2 and their ages; a raw observed epoch; last received completed-lap duration; retained prior positive sector groups with source clocks and counts; as-of pit/session/track flags; and optional candidate-stint fields explicitly separated from canonical tyre age. Field aggregates may consume only strictly earlier updates. Historical track/driver priors must come from races completed before the forecast race. Unsupported fields get missingness/count indicators and a predeclared fallback, not future CSV imputation.

Two credible same-checkpoint point references should precede any learned residual claim: (a) observed sector sum plus robust past-only own-driver remaining-sector estimates, with a historical track prior for cold starts; (b) a shrinkage version combining own-driver and strictly earlier field sector estimates, conditioned on observed track/pit regime. The sum predicts the full lap duration; it is not itself a remaining-time target. Because the requested next eligible target may skip the current lap, a calibration or empirical-mixture layer must be learned from prior races only and applied from prefix features, without using eventual current-lap cleanliness to choose its branch. Compare the learner to these references on identical issuance/target pairs. Any carried production HGB point belongs only in a separately labeled recorded-reference diagnostic until its own receipt/input contract is certified. A gain at S2 would not improve the original lap-boundary forecasting horizon by definition.
