**What the mathematics measures.** For F1 point forecasts, each event has weight one: first average absolute errors within event, then average those event errors. If event e contains n_e matched targets, the reported loss is mean_e[sum_i |prediction_ei − outcome_ei| / n_e]. A long race therefore does not automatically dominate a short one. Rank MAE uses a legal full-field permutation; best-lap and live MAE are in seconds on explicitly matched finite populations.

For football, log loss is −mean_i log(p_i,outcome), using natural logarithms; Brier loss is mean_i sum_k(p_ik − 1[outcome_i=k])², summed over all three classes. These assess probabilities, whereas accuracy evaluates only their largest component. Proper scoring motivates this distinction; lower log loss alone does not establish improved calibration or a trading edge. [Gneiting and Raftery, 2007](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf).

All interval tables use candidate-minus-baseline loss, so negative values favor the candidate. They are paired historical bootstrap intervals. They are not posterior probabilities of success, do not repair historical research exposure or model-selection multiplicity, and cannot turn thousands of correlated laps into thousands of independent races. Relative reductions divide by the observed baseline mean. The intervals below are for absolute paired loss differences, not independent uncertainty estimates for percentage ratios.

**Live F1: a small correction works historically, with weaker recent transfer.** The preferred model predicts the last observed eligible lap plus a clipped ridge correction. Training residuals are clipped to ±3 seconds; standardized features describe local pace, clipped trend, strictly earlier other-driver changes, stint/tyre state and observation gaps. The fitted correction is clipped to ±2 seconds. Ridge minimizes weighted squared residual error plus alpha times the squared coefficient norm; intercept is unpenalized. Weights are N/(E n_e), giving each training event equal total weight while summing to N. Alpha 100 was selected on 2023 after training on 2022. Coefficients and preprocessing were then fitted once on 2022–23 and frozen.

This is a deliberately restricted bias correction. Squared-error fitting targets a conditional mean, whereas MAE favors a conditional median; clipping changes that estimation target further. The experiment demonstrates an empirical comparison, not a proof of an MAE-optimal estimator. A recent state-space paper supplied a useful mechanism to investigate, but its one-driver Austrian-GP illustration does not establish cross-event performance for this repository. [Cappello and Hoegh, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00640).

**Football: weighting recent results and mixing distinct probability models.** The preferred forecast is the arithmetic mixture p = 0.5 p_DC365 + 0.5 p_Elo. The DC component exponentially weights its likelihood by 2^(−age_days/365); the baseline uses equal match weights. Both use the same prior 1,095-calendar-day fit population. Causal Elo uses initial rating 1,500, K 20, home advantage 60 and base-10 scale 400. A regularized multinomial mapping converts prior rating difference and its absolute value into home/draw/away probabilities. No odds or same-match statistics enter these models.

Warmup covers 2017/18–2021/22. Two EPL validation seasons select the mixture before the 2024/25–2025/26 test labels are loaded. Hyperparameters, mixture weight and the two-month refit schedule stay fixed; fitted coefficients are re-estimated from prior results, including already completed earlier test matches. Every prediction is made at local day start, results become available at the next local midnight, and same-day Elo updates occur together after predictions. This explicitly handles daylight-saving changes. Source files and field definitions come directly from [Football-Data's England archive](https://football-data.co.uk/englandm.php) and [schema notes](https://football-data.co.uk/notes.txt).

**Pre-event F1: large older gains fail the current-regime check.** Q1 combines normalized ranks using fixed weights: 65% latest practice, 20% earlier practice and 15% prior form on standard weekends; 85% Sprint Qualifying and 15% history on supported sprint weekends. Q2 fits a ridge residual with alpha 10 to prior events. Both produce permutations by stable sorting scores. This is a practical rank estimator, not a proof of the Bayes-optimal permutation under absolute rank loss.

The Q2 historical delta is −0.389583 positions, with three-event circular-block interval [−0.581250, −0.243750]. It improves 38 of 48 events. In exposed 2026 it instead adds 0.141414 positions of error, interval [−0.010101, +0.333333]. The rehearsal baseline is already much stronger in 2026: 1.868687 versus 3.298355 positions historically. The experiment cannot attribute that shift uniquely to car regulations, source quality or event mix; treating it as identified causation would exceed the evidence.

Best-lap L1 scales same-season source shifts by rehearsal duration. L2 fits prior contextual residuals in percent of rehearsal duration with Huber regression, epsilon 1.35 and alpha 1, then returns seconds. Its 26.197% historical gain is dominated by three wet-rehearsal events: baseline 10.410650 → 3.572380 seconds there, compared with 1.396802 → 1.304943 across the other 45 events. The weather grouping is a post-scoring diagnosis, not a selected forecast rule. L2 worsens exposed 2026 from 0.513577 to 0.595635 seconds. More training flexibility would not by itself solve this transfer failure.

Coverage is 92 of 101 cached weekends; nine older sprint formats lack the causal rehearsal expected by this head. Primary qualifying has 959 roster rows and best-lap scoring has 936 matched rows. Missing clean anchors or target laps constrain that population. The baseline lap shift expands within season and differs from the separately frozen object in the earlier audit. See the [pre-event README](../../research/experiments/performance_20260907/pre_event/README.md).

**The next research path follows the transfer failures.** First priority is a frozen EPL-specific challenger evaluated at the same day-start horizon on subsequent observations, with log loss primary and calibration tracked separately. Second is a narrowly specified live adaptation experiment, comparing the unchanged ridge and naive policies against a single causally updated correction; any new tuning belongs to a fresh cycle, because the four new races are now exposed. Third is pre-event source and weather adaptation, requiring explicit inference-time state definitions and evaluation beyond the exposed 2026 block. Broadening race-order model complexity has the weakest evidence here.

A future promotion decision should require a predeclared temporal test, improvement in its primary paired loss, stability across relevant seasons or regimes, and no unacceptable probability or coverage regression. Numerical thresholds and stopping rules must be fixed before that new test is scored. This cycle demonstrates where performance gains exist and where they fail to transfer; it does not establish prospective lift, calibrated F1 uncertainty, survival-head improvement or betting profitability. Those outcomes were not tested.
